How to claim your unpaid dividend

Two honest options: do it yourself for free using the steps below, or ask a registered professional to do it for you for a fee. Start by finding out which stage your money is at.

Please read - conditions and limits. We wrote these steps on a best-efforts basis from public rules (Companies Act 2013 s.124-125, IEPF Rules 2016) and common company practice. Procedures, forms and document lists change and differ between companies and registrars, so we cannot promise they are complete or fully current. Please do your own research and confirm with the company's registrar (RTA) and iepf.gov.in before you act. Nothing here is legal, tax or investment advice, and DhanKhoj does not guarantee that any claim will be accepted or paid.

Step 0 - Which stage is your money at?

Route A

Under 7 years since it became unpaid

Still with the company's Unpaid Dividend Account. Claim from the company / its registrar. Usually faster.

Route B

7 years or more

Transferred to the Investor Education and Protection Fund (IEPF). The company list no longer shows it. Claim from the IEPF with Form IEPF-5. Longer, paper-heavy.

The deadline on your dashboard is an estimate. Ask the company for the exact transfer date. If the date is close, start Route A immediately.

Route A - claim from the company (free)

  1. Confirm the entry is yoursMatch the folio / DP-Client ID, name and number of shares with your own share certificates, demat statement or old dividend warrants. A same-name stranger is possible.
  2. Find the registrarOn the company's website (Investors / Shareholder services) find the Registrar & Transfer Agent (RTA) - for example KFin, MUFG Intime, Bigshare. Note its email and postal address.
  3. Gather documentsTypically: PAN copy, ID and address proof, cancelled cheque or bank-statement page showing name and account number, demat client master (CML) if shares are demat, and for old physical folios the share certificate copy. Your registrar's list governs.
  4. Write the claim letter and formsA signed letter quoting folio, name, shares and the dividend year, plus any registrar forms (KYC / bank-update forms such as ISR-1, ISR-2, ISR-3 where they ask for them).
  5. Send and keep proofEmail and post it. Keep courier receipts and email copies. Note the reference number you are given.
  6. Follow upAsk for status after 2-3 weeks. The payment normally arrives in your bank account; some companies pay by cheque / demand draft.
  7. If refused or ignoredEscalate in writing to the company's Investor Grievance / Compliance Officer, then file a complaint on SEBI SCORES (scores.sebi.gov.in).

Route B - claim from the IEPF with Form IEPF-5 (free)

  1. Collect details from the company or RTAFolio / DP-Client ID, number of shares, dividend amount, year(s) and, if available, the IEPF transfer date and the company's CIN. Ask the company's nodal officer for a statement of what was transferred.
  2. Register and fill Form IEPF-5 onlineOn iepf.gov.in (MCA portal). Enter shareholder, bank and claim details accurately; the bank account must be in your name.
  3. Upload and submitAttach the documents the form asks for, then submit and note the Service Request Number (SRN).
  4. Print, sign and post the hard-copy setSend the signed form with the listed documents (ID, bank proof, indemnity bond where required, transfer proof etc.) to the company's nodal officer as per the form's instructions.
  5. Company verifiesThe company checks the claim and submits its verification report on the portal (the rules give it a limited time; in practice, follow up).
  6. IEPF Authority decides and paysApproved dividends are paid into your Aadhaar-linked / named bank account; shares are credited to your demat account. Track the SRN on the portal.
  7. Legal heirsIf the shareholder has died, extra documents are needed (death certificate, succession / legal-heir proof, nominee details). Expect a longer process.

Pitfalls to avoid

  • Name or signature differences between old records and today's ID - fix KYC first.
  • Bank account not in the claimant's name, or not active.
  • Missing folio details or wrong company CIN.
  • Never share OTPs, net-banking passwords or card details with anyone claiming to "release" your dividend.
